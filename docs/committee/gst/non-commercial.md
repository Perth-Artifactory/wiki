Some supplies that would otherwise incur GST can be treated as GST free if they are determined to be a non-commerical supply. For a supply to be non-commercial it needs to be less than 75% of the cost of supply[^1] (pretty much nothing we currently do) or less than 50% of the market value of the supply[^2] provided by for-profit organisations[^7]. In order to determine the market value of a supply we rely on one of three successive[^6] tests:

1. Same supply[^3] - Where an identical supply can be found (considering the factors below)
2. Similar supply[^4] - Where an identical supply cannot be found (considering the factors below. In this circumstance the primary factor that can be expanded is the market in which supply exists.
3. The approved 'cost plus' method[^5] - While allowed under the guidelines to date we have not used this test and have instead chosen to accept the lost gain.

[^1]: [The non-commercial supply rules listed in section 38-250 mean that anything supplied by a charity is GST-free if the payment, or consideration for the supply is either: ... less than 75% of the 'cost of supply' under subsection 38-250(2).](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=less%20than%2075%25%20of%20the%20%27cost%20of%20supply%27%20under%20subsection%2038%2D250(2).)
[^2]: [a supply of something other than accommodation and the consideration for the supply is less than 50% of the GST-inclusive market value of the supply.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=a%20supply%20of%20something%20other%20than%20accommodation%20and%20the%20consideration%20for%20the%20supply%20is%20less%20than%2050%25%20of%20the%20GST%2Dinclusive%20market%20value%20of%20the%20supply)
[^3]: [The same supply test requires a charity to work out whether a supply, the same as the one it makes, exists within the market they operate in.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=The%20same%20supply%20test%20requires%20a%20charity%20to%20work%20out%20whether%20a%20supply%2C%20the%20same%20as%20the%20one%20it%20makes%2C%20exists%20within%20the%20market%20they%20operate%20in%2E)
[^4]: [If there is no same supply in the market, the charity must work out whether a 'similar supply' exists.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=If%20there%20is%20no%20same%20supply%20in%20the%20market%2C%20the%20charity%20must%20work%20out%20whether%20a%20%27similar%20supply%27%20exists)
[^5]: [The Commissioner has approved the use by charities of the following 'cost plus' method to work out the market value of a supply. Charities can only use this method if they cannot identify a same or similar supply to the one it makes.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=109%2E-,The,makes)

For all interactions compared the exchange must be between:

* a knowledgeable, willing and not anxious buyer, and
* a knowledgeable, willing and not anxious seller acting at 'arm's length' in an appropriate market.

Where possible multiple comparisons should be sought[^8]. Private guidance from the ATO has indicated that 3-4 options is appropriate.

Comparisons must be documented[^10] and rechecked yearly[^9] or when costs change. We have largely opted to pin this check to financial years for ease of scheduling.

[^8]: [In the market the charity operates in, there may be more than one other supplier making the same supply but for a different price. Whilst it is not practical to obtain the full range of prices (of the same supply) the charity should generally obtain more than one price.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=87%2E-,In,price%2E,-3)
[^9]: [This information should be reviewed regularly, for example, yearly or when prices or costs change.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=This%20information%20should%20be%20reviewed%20regularly%2C%20for%20example%2C%20yearly%20or%20when%20prices%20or%20costs%20change%2E)
[^10]: (Charities must keep and maintain records that adequately document the process and information collected in working out the relevant market values which the consideration of the supplies they make is being compared to.)[https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=193%2E-,Charities,to]

For each comparison a number of factors must be considered:

## Market

For a same supply test the comparisons must be in the same market as the organisation.[^11] Where supply within the same market cannot be identified we must move on to a similar test and identify a broader applicable market segment[^12]. When considering a broader market it is worth referring to the examples provided by the ATO[^13] rather than this internal page.

Market examples:

* Second hand goods vs retail new[^14] - not the same or similar market
* Second hand goods sold by the organisation vs second hand retailer (e.g. cash converters) - same market[^15]
* Purchasing products and reselling at the same location - same market[^16]
* Soldering workshops vs other skill building workshops - broadly similar market provided other aspects of the supply (ie quality of the delivery) are the same[^17]

[^11]: [Charities must ensure they make their comparison in the same market they operate in.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=Charities%20must%20ensure%20they%20make%20their%20comparison%20in%20the%20same%20market%20they%20operate%20in%2E)
[^12]: [For some supplies, charities may have difficulties in identifying a supply similar to the one they make in the market. In order to reduce compliance costs, a charity can use a broad categorisation of the supply it makes as justification for using certain supplies in the market as similar supplies.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=92%2E-,For,supplies,-%2E)
[^13]: [Similiar supply: Identifying the market](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=Identifying%20the%20market,-92)

## Locality of supply

The geographic locality of the supply may be an important factor in the comparison. Specifically where the area significantly affects the price[^18].

Note: While in general the ATO considers food to be an example of a supply that does not have a significant geographic factor[^19] they have determined that our assertion that industrial vs residential/light commercial zoning plays a significant factor in the cost of soft drink is appropriate.[^20]

[^18]: [Therefore the area the market covers or the locality of the supplies can be an important factor in working out whether the comparison is acceptable. This factor is clear when there is a strong correlation between the geographic location and the price that can be charged.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=Therefore%20the,charged)
[^19]: [Food is an example of the type of supplies where the geographic location of the market or supply may not be a material factor in the 'same supply test'.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=Food%20is%20an%20example%20of%20the%20type%20of%20supplies%20where%20the%20geographic%20location%20of%20the%20market%20or%20supply%20may%20not%20be%20a%20material%20factor%20in%20the%20%27same%20supply%20test%27%2E)
[^20]: Written correspondence dated 16th September 2025 between Fletcher Boyd (Perth Artifactory Inc.) and James Fennelly (ATO Law Interpretation Officer: Technical Leadership and Advice- GST)

## Quality/nature of supply

For the same supply test, the supply must have the same quality or nature as the supplies in the market it is being compared with. Quality may include such things as:

* Brands (e.g. Arduino Nano vs "Arduino" Nano would not be the same)
* Durability, functionality, workmanship and reliability (3D printed vs 3D printed is fine)
* Expertise[^21]
* Facilities
* Fashion
* Materials, packaging or surrounding
* Obsolescence
* Professionalism
* Technology
* Warranty

[^21]: [The school cannot use a supply to attend a performance of the same play performed by a professional theatre company](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=The%20school%20cannot%20use%20a%20supply%20to%20attend%20a%20performance%20of%20the%20same%20play%20performed%20by%20a%20professional%20theatre%20company)

Where we are unable to find an item in the same market of similar quality (ie others are better) we can take steps to scale the price.[^22] Four steps must be completed:

* Identify the 'same' supply and its price
* Identify all the differing characteristics shown in the quality of the 'same supply' when comparing with the supply the charity makes
* Quantify those differing characteristics on a reasonable basis, and
* Use the quantified values of those differing characteristics to adjust the price of the 'same supply'.

We must maintain documentary evidence of conclusive and tangible proof of each step.[^23]

Note: Nothing in the guidance specifies the direction in which a price is scaled but the underlying purchaser characteristics requirements still apply.[^24]

[^22]: [In a case where there are genuinely identifiable differences in the quality of the 'same supply', the charity needs to take into account those differences in establishing the market value of the supply it makes.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=68%2E-,In,makes%2E)
[^23]: [Charities must maintain documentary evidence of conclusive and tangible proof for each of these steps.](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=must%20maintain%20documentary%20evidence%20of%20conclusive%20and%20tangible%20proof%20for%20each%20of%20these%20steps)
[^24]: [The market value determined must represent an amount a willing but not anxious purchaser is prepared to pay. ](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=The%20market%20value%20determined%20must%20represent%20an%20amount%20a%20willing%20but%20not%20anxious%20purchaser%20is%20prepared%20to%20pay%2E)

## Size, quantity, or duration of supply

The size, quantity or duration of a thing supplied are factors which have a bearing on the market value of the supply.[^25]

We cannot use the inverse scale of bulk supplies to our advantage when we supply the bulk quantity. e.g. our 24 pack of widgets cannot be compared against individually sold widgets to get to justify a market value of (competitor widget x 24)

However, the ATO has indicated that when determining a similar supply it may be appropriate in some circumstances to compare a comparison's weekly price x 4 vs our monthly price within reason. In particular where that adjustment puts the monthly value within the range of other options on the market.[^20]

[^25]: [The size, quantity or duration of a thing supplied are factors which have a bearing on the market value of the supply. ](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GII/GSTIICC2/NAT/ATO/00001#:~:text=The%20size%2C%20quantity%20or%20duration%20of%20a%20thing%20supplied%20are%20factors%20which%20have%20a%20bearing%20on%20the%20market%20value%20of%20the%20supply%2E)

## Condition of supply

Any conditions placed on the supply must be factored in to the comparison. This can include the quantity/duration of the supply[^26]
